What counts as a number in this work

The single biggest thing separating a resume that gets a call from one that does not is whether the claims can be checked. In this field, these are the figures that do it:

  • downtime you cut, in hours or percent
  • feet of conduit, cable tray or pipe run
  • voltage and system size you worked on
  • incident free hours or days on site
  • square footage of the facility
  • units, machines or panels you maintained

What it looks for

Credentials

  • Journeyman or master license
  • OSHA 10 or OSHA 30
  • CDL
  • AWS D1.1
  • EPA 608
  • apprenticeship hours

Missing any of these when the posting asks for them costs you points, and often costs you the screen.

Strong verbs for this field

  • fabricated
  • installed
  • inspected
  • calibrated
  • welded
  • wired
  • rigged
  • commissioned
  • serviced

Bullets that open on one of these read as work you did. Bullets that open on "Responsible for" read as a job description.

The mistake almost everyone makes

Trade resumes get judged on the same rules as everyone else, which is unfair but real. The winning move is numbers, because your work produces them naturally. Downtime, footage, voltage, incident free hours. Most people leave all of it off.
