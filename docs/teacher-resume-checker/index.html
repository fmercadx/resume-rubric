What counts as a number in this work

The single biggest thing separating a resume that gets a call from one that does not is whether the claims can be checked. In this field, these are the figures that do it:

  • class size and number of sections
  • growth on a assessment, in points or percent
  • pass rates or proficiency rates you moved
  • students served across a year
  • attendance or behavior referrals you reduced
  • colleagues you mentored or trained

What it looks for

Credentials

  • State teaching certificate
  • endorsements
  • Praxis
  • ESL or SPED certification
  • master's degree

Missing any of these when the posting asks for them costs you points, and often costs you the screen.

Strong verbs for this field

  • differentiated
  • assessed
  • facilitated
  • mentored
  • scaffolded
  • designed
  • tutored

Bullets that open on one of these read as work you did. Bullets that open on "Responsible for" read as a job description.

The mistake almost everyone makes

Teaching resumes drift into philosophy. Districts screen for evidence. One line with growth data beats a paragraph about your approach to learning, because the first can be checked and the second cannot.
