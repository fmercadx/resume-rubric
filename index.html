The loop

Five passes over one document.

01 / 05

Read

Resume Rubric splits your resume into a contact block, section headers, roles, and individual bullets. Anything it cannot parse is exactly what an applicant tracking system cannot parse either, so it gets flagged first.

  • Finds your sections even when the headings are unusual
  • Separates real bullets from headers, dates, and address lines
  • Flags the formatting that silently breaks resume parsers
02 / 05

Extract

The posting gets mined for the terms that actually carry weight. Single words, multi word phrases, tools, credentials, and certifications, ranked by how often and how prominently the employer used them.

  • Weights terms in the requirements section above the boilerplate
  • Understands aliases: RN and registered nurse count as one term
  • Ignores the legal and equal opportunity filler at the bottom
03 / 05

Score

Six dimensions, one hundred points, and a citation for every deduction. You never get a number without the line of your resume that produced it.

  • Match, impact, voice, parseability, writing, completeness
  • Field aware: a welder is not graded like a product manager
  • The full weighting is published, not hidden behind a signup
04 / 05

Rewrite

Weak bullets get restructured into strong verb, concrete object, method, and result. Missing keywords get slotted where they honestly fit. Where a number belongs and you have not given one, you get a labelled blank instead of a fabrication.

  • Kills "responsible for", passive voice, and first person
  • Upgrades vague verbs against a field specific lexicon
  • Marks every place a metric belongs, and never invents one
05 / 05

Ship

Compare the original and the rewrite side by side with every change highlighted, generate a matching cover letter built only from facts already in your resume, then save the whole report as a PDF.

  • Word level diff so you can accept changes one at a time
  • Cover letter drafted from your real bullets, not a template
  • Printable report you can hand to a mentor or a career coach
Open book

Here is the entire rubric.

Every other tool gives you a score and hides the formula. A score you cannot audit is a score you cannot act on. This is the whole thing.

DimensionPointsWhat is measuredFull marks when
Job matchThe keyword screen 30 Frequency weighted terms and phrases pulled from the posting, checked against your resume with alias resolution. You cover the employer's high weight terms honestly, in context, in your own bullets.
Quantified impactThe credibility test 20 Share of bullets containing a number, percentage, currency figure, duration, or countable volume. At least three in five bullets carry a real, verifiable figure.
Action & voiceThe first three words 15 Opening verb strength, weak stems like "responsible for", passive constructions, and first person pronouns. Every bullet opens on a strong past tense verb with no pronouns anywhere.
ATS parseabilityWill a machine read it 15 Contact block, recognizable section headers, date consistency, character hygiene, and total length. A parser can find your email, your sections, and your dates without guessing.
Writing qualityThe human screen 10 Filler words, unbacked buzzwords, repeated verbs, and bullet length distribution. No filler, no unearned adjectives, and varied bullets in the eight to thirty word range.
CompletenessThe obvious gaps 10 Required sections, employment dates, education, and the credentials your specific field expects. Nothing a recruiter would have to email you to ask for is missing.

Questions

Answers, briefly.

Does my resume really stay on my device?

Yes, and you can verify it yourself. Open your browser's developer tools, go to the network tab, and run an analysis. There are no requests, because there is no server. The entire engine, every lexicon and every rule, is in the page you already downloaded. Disconnect from the internet and everything except the job feed still works.

The job feed uses the network. What exactly does it send?

The name of a job board, and nothing else. It is a plain request for a company's public list of openings, the same list anyone can load on their careers page, and it happens only when you press Fetch. Your resume is not part of that request: the postings come back first, and the matching runs here afterward. That is also why the feed can score every role against you without any company learning that you looked.

Why is there no LinkedIn or Indeed in the job feed?

Because both prohibit it. Their terms forbid automated collection, they block it in practice, and a tool that quietly breaks those rules on your behalf is not one you should trust with your resume either. What the feed uses instead are the job board APIs that Greenhouse, Ashby, and Lever publish specifically so that postings can be embedded and syndicated. Those are meant to be read. The tradeoff is honest coverage: strong for companies using those systems, thinner for employers who post only to a job site or their own portal.

How can it score anything without an AI model?

Because most of what an applicant tracking system does is not intelligent. It matches keywords, checks for parseable structure, and ranks. Resume Rubric reproduces that mechanically, then applies the writing rules that human recruiters have used for decades: lead with a verb, attach a number, cut the filler. A fixed set of rules has one big advantage: it can show you exactly why it took a point off.

Why will it not write metrics into my bullets?

Because you have to sit in the interview afterward. A tool that turns "managed the schedule" into "managed the schedule, reducing overtime 34%" has just written a claim you cannot defend. Resume Rubric marks the slot, tells you what kind of figure belongs there, and stops. The blank is the point.

Will the rewritten bullets sound like a machine wrote them?

The rewriter restructures your own words rather than generating new prose. It moves the verb to the front, strips the padding, and puts your outcome at the end. Nothing is invented, so nothing has that hollow generated texture. You should still read every line and change anything that does not sound like you.

My field is not on the list. Does it still work?

Yes. Leave the selector on "General" and every universal rule still applies, since verbs, numbers, structure, and parseability matter everywhere. The field profiles only add credential checks and a specialized verb lexicon on top.

Should I upload my resume or paste it?

Upload it. Not only because dragging a file beats copy and paste, but because it measures the right thing. An applicant tracking system parses the file you send, and when you copy out of a PDF viewer the viewer has already done that extraction for you, quietly repairing the damage. Pasting therefore hides the parsing faults the score is meant to catch. Both PDF and .docx are unpacked here in the page, with no upload to anywhere. If a file cannot be decoded cleanly you get told exactly what went wrong instead of a score computed on scrambled text, because a file this cannot read is usually a file a resume screen cannot read either.

Can I get the report out of the browser?

Use "Save report as PDF" after an analysis. It opens your browser's print dialog with a layout built for paper, where every section is expanded and the navigation is stripped out. Choose "Save as PDF" as the destination.

Is the score the same thing a real ATS would give me?

No, and be suspicious of anyone claiming otherwise. Workday, Greenhouse, Taleo, and iCIMS each rank differently, and none of them publish their formula. What Resume Rubric scores is the set of things every one of them is known to check, plus the writing standards a human applies after the machine passes you through. Treat it as a rehearsal, not a verdict.
